About

I grew up in Portugal and now call Dublin home. My path from writing my first lines of code to working as Chief Architect at Mews has been shaped by a simple realization: the hardest part of building software is almost never the code itself—it’s how systems, teams, and human habits fit together.

I'm that person who still gets a genuine kick out of an elegant refactor, the quiet satisfaction of deleting 2,000 lines of redundant code, or drawing an architecture diagram that makes a complex system suddenly feel obvious. I believe great architecture isn't about imposing rules from an ivory tower; it's about clearing friction so engineers can do their best work without feeling like they're fighting the system.

These days, I spend much of my time thinking about platform evolution, distributed systems, and what the rise of AI tools means for the craft of engineering. When machines can generate code in seconds, human discernment, taste, and architectural judgment matter more than ever.

Writing is how I untangle my own thinking. Most of my essays on blog.zepedro.com rely on metaphors—looking at engineering through police sketch artists, wild gardens, old kitchens, and cartographers who stopped traveling. When I’m not sketching systems or writing, you’ll find me tinkering, drinking far too much espresso, and enjoying life on a rainy Dublin afternoon.
